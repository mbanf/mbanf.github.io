## The question

Both dispersal limitation and environmental sorting can shape genetic variation within a species, but their relative contribution is poorly understood for microbes. Nectar yeasts are a good system: discrete habitats (flowers), strong competition between strains, measurable traits.

## What we did

We sequenced the genomes of 102 strains of *M. reukaufii* isolated from the floral nectar of *Mimulus aurantiacus* along a 200 km stretch of Californian coast, and related genotypic diversity to geographic distance and to phenotypic traits — morphology, metabolic ability and interspecific competitiveness.

## What we found

Intraspecific genetic variation showed no detectable relationship with distance. It did fall into three distinct lineages, and those lineages correlated with metabolic ability and competitiveness. Yet the full spectrum of genotypic and phenotypic diversity seen across the whole coastline was present even within 200 m, and more competitive strains were not necessarily more abundant. Neither dispersal limitation nor environmental sorting fully explains the diversity — a reminder that microbial population genetics needs more than the two usual suspects.
